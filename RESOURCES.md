# Resources for Kmhmu' / Khmu Language Work

Reference material to check new dictionary entries against, and background for anyone
working on Kmhmu' language tools. Kmhmu' is also romanized Khmu, Khmu', Kammu, or Khamu
(ISO 639-3: `kjg`), part of the Khmuic branch of Austroasiatic, spoken across Laos and
parts of Thailand, Vietnam, and China, with significant dialect variation and no single
standard written form.

## Language identifiers & catalogs

- **[Ethnologue: Khmu (kjg)](https://www.ethnologue.com/language/kjg/)** — population,
  dialects, official status.
- **[Glottolog: Khmu](https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/id/khmu1256)** — genealogical
  classification, dialect list, bibliography.
- **[ISO 639-3: kjg](https://iso639-3.sil.org/code/kjg)**
- **[ScriptSource: Khmu](https://scriptsource.org/cms/scripts/page.php?item_id=language_detail&key=kjg)**
  — writing systems in use.
- **[Wikipedia: Khmu language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmu_language)** — general
  overview, dialect map, phonology summary.

## Published dictionaries (the gold-standard cross-check)

Suwilai Premsrirat (Research Institute for Language and Culture of Asia, Mahidol
University) produced the major modern reference series — a dictionary per country:

- *Dictionary of Khmu in Laos* (Mon-Khmer Studies Special Publication No. 1, Vol. 3), 2002
- *Dictionary of Khmu in Thailand*
- *Dictionary of Khmu in Vietnam*
- *Dictionary of Khmu in China* (with Robert S. Bauer, Huang Ying, Saowaphak
  Woralakkhanakul)
- Plus a companion *Thesaurus of Khmu Dialects in Southeast Asia*

Print copies turn up secondhand via AbeBooks/Amazon. A **free sample** — the
morphosyntax front-matter from *Dictionary of Khmu in Laos*, with real example sentences —
is on the Internet Archive via the Rosetta Project:
[archive.org/details/rosettaproject_kjg_morsyn-1](https://archive.org/details/rosettaproject_kjg_morsyn-1)
(PDF/full-text download). It's a grammar sketch, not the full A–Z wordlist, but it's a
legitimate, citable academic source and worth reading before relying on the print
dictionaries.

Dr. Premsrirat's RILCA center at Mahidol also publishes a
[Thai–Khmu–English Dictionary](https://lc.mahidol.ac.th/en/publication/thai-khmu-english-dictionary/).

## Orthography & typing tools

- **SIL Kmhmu Keyman keyboard** (v1.5, © SIL International) — a Lao-script keyboard layout
  purpose-built for Kmhmu', plus the matching **Kmhmu MX** and **Kmhmu OT** fonts. It
  covers characters Kmhmu' needs that standard Lao fonts don't reliably support, including
  the `ໞ`/`ໟ` marks (used in some pending/proposed entries — not yet in `kmhmudict.txt` on
  `master`). Download it from [SIL's Keyman keyboard listing](https://keyman.com/keyboards/)
  — search "Kmhmu"; it isn't checked into this repo.
  **This is the standard to check spelling/character usage against** — if a submitted
  entry uses characters this keyboard doesn't produce, ask the contributor about it before
  merging.

## People / community

- Kmhmu' dialects differ enough (Rok, Yuan, Lu, and others) that a word confirmed by one
  speaker may need a dialect note rather than be treated as universal. Always capture
  dialect/region per `CONTRIBUTING.md`.
- If you have contacts in Kmhmu' community groups (e.g. Kmhmu' cultural associations,
  diaspora community pages), they're often the fastest way to get a second confirmation
  on a word a published dictionary doesn't cover — faster than waiting on a library copy.
